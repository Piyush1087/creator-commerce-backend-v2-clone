# Collaboration experience guide

This guide describes how brand–creator collaborations are meant to work on the platform: what each side sees, what happens at each step, and the rules that keep deals fair and predictable.

It reflects the approved product specification for the **Universal Collaboration Engine** (brand and creator views stay in sync).

---

## What a collaboration is

A **collaboration** is a single working relationship between one **brand** and one **creator** on one **campaign** and one **brief**. It is not the same as the campaign “pipeline” list (prospects and applicants). The collaboration journey usually starts after the brand **approves** the creator on a campaign.

Both sides work in one shared place:

- A **conversation** (messages and system updates, similar to a messaging app).
- An **execution panel** (the step-by-step actions required right now—review quote, fund deal, ship product, approve content, and so on).

On desktop, these appear side by side with a list of all active collaborations. On mobile, the flow is: pick a conversation → read the chat → open the action panel.

---

## Who does what

| Role | Main responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| **Brand** | Approve creators into a collaboration, agree on price, fund or confirm payment, send product or access, review drafts, verify the live post, pay the balance, leave a rating. |
| **Creator** | Propose (or accept) price, provide bank details when needed, confirm receipt of product or access, submit script or content, publish, submit the live link, leave a rating. |
| **Platform** | Enforces limits (negotiation rounds, revision rounds, delivery strikes), runs timers (e.g. 72-hour content review), splits payments 30% / 70%, records every step in the chat as a system message. |

---

## How a collaboration starts

1. The brand runs a campaign and receives **applications** (or invites prospects).
2. When the brand **approves** a creator for that campaign, a collaboration thread opens for that brand–creator pair.
3. The creator typically sees a welcome message in the chat, e.g. that they are approved and should review the brief and secure their spot.
4. The collaboration begins at **Step 1: Negotiation**, unless the campaign type skips negotiation (see below).

---

## Payment modes (how money moves)

Every collaboration uses one of three modes. The mode affects which screens appear and whether cash changes hands.

| Mode | In plain terms |
| --- | --- |
| **Escrow** | The brand pays the full agreed amount into a platform-held vault. The platform releases **30%** after funding (advance) and **70%** after the brand verifies the live post (or equivalent rules). |
| **Manual** | The brand pays the creator directly (bank transfer). The brand uploads proof of the advance; the creator confirms they received it. Bank details must be on file before manual deals proceed. |
| **Barter** | No cash fee: value is product-only or equivalent. Cash quote is zero. **Step 2 (Securement)** is skipped; the flow moves straight to logistics when terms are agreed. |

For all paid deals, the platform shows the split clearly: **30% advance**, **70% final balance**, based on the **total quote** the parties agree on.

---

## The six steps (end-to-end)

Each collaboration moves through six steps. Only one step is “active” at a time. The chat and execution panel always reflect that step.

```text
Step 1  Negotiation     → Agree on price and scope
Step 2  Securement      → Lock the deal (fund or confirm payment / contract)
Step 3  Logistics       → Product, access, or codes so the creator can create
Step 4  Content         → Script (if needed) and media drafts, brand review
Step 5  Publishing      → Live post on social and compliance check
Step 6  Feedback        → Ratings, archive, wrap-up
```

---

### Step 1 — Negotiation

**Goal:** Agree on the total cash fee (if any), understand deliverables and usage rights, and see any complimentary product value.

**What the creator sees**

- Read-only sections: sample product (if any), deliverables (e.g. number of reels/stories), usage rights (ads, reposting, link-in-bio)—only what the brand actually requested in the brief.
- A place to enter **their quote** (for negotiable campaigns), or accept a **fixed** fee, or accept **barter** with no cash field.

**What the brand sees**

- The creator’s quote compared to campaign budget guidance.
- The same read-only scope so the brand only reviews what they asked for—nothing extra.
- Actions: **Accept**, **Send counter-offer** (one amount), or **Decline**.

**Negotiation limit (“one-strike”)**

- The creator opens with an **initial quote**.
- The brand may **counter once**.
- The creator may then **accept the brand’s price** or send **one final offer** (marked as final—no more counters).
- After that, the brand can only **accept** or **decline**—not negotiate further.

**When terms are agreed**

- The chat shows a system message that commercials are locked (amount and brief summary).
- Everyone moves to **Step 2**, except **barter** deals skip Step 2.

**Campaign type shortcuts**

| Campaign type | Negotiation behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| **Negotiable** | Full quote / counter / final offer flow above. |
| **Fixed** | Creator mainly accepts or declines the preset fee. |
| **Barter** | No cash quote; skip heavy negotiation and often skip Step 2. |

---

### Step 2 — Securement

**Goal:** Make the deal binding—money in place or payment obligation acknowledged—before work and shipping proceed.

**Escrow**

- The brand adds enough funds to cover the agreed total.
- When funding is complete, the platform treats the vault as funded and can release the **30% advance** to the creator per policy.
- The chat notes that the deal is secured and logistics can start.

**Manual**

- The creator must have **bank details** saved; otherwise the brand sees a “waiting for bank details” state and can nudge the creator.
- The brand confirms liability for the **30% advance** and later uploads a **receipt** for that transfer.
- The creator confirms they received the advance before logistics unlock.

**Barter**

- This step is **skipped** after Step 1; the system moves directly to logistics.

**Agreement**

- A collaboration summary (brief, quote, terms) is intended to be generated and stored for records when the brand completes securement actions.

---

### Step 3 — Logistics and fulfillment

**Goal:** The creator has everything needed to produce content—physical product, digital access, or visit/code as required.

**What changes by industry**

| Brand type | Typical logistics |
| --- | --- |
| **D2C / product** | Brand ships a sample; tracking number is required when dispatching. Creator confirms **received**. |
| **SaaS / digital** | Brand provides credentials or access links. |
| **Healthcare / offline** | Redemption codes, appointments, or visit scheduling as defined in the brief. |

**Creator**

- May need to confirm a **shipping address** before dispatch.
- Confirms **“I received it”** (or equivalent for digital access). Until they do, **uploading content in Step 4 stays locked**.

**Brand**

- Dispatches product or shares access.
- If the creator reports a problem (damaged, lost, invalid code, no-show, etc.), the brand is prompted to fix it.

**Two-strike rule (logistics)**

- If the creator reports a **serious fulfillment problem twice** and still has not confirmed successful receipt, the collaboration hits a **logistics deadlock**.
- The platform steers toward **cancellation** and refund of held funds (escrow) where applicable.
- Manual-payment deals may require the parties to coordinate reversal of an advance outside the platform.

**Skipping Step 3**

- If there is **no complimentary sample** and the brief is purely digital with no shipment, Step 3 may be minimal or skipped after securement.

---

### Step 4 — Content production and review

**Goal:** Align on creative (script if needed), then approve the final media before posting.

**Script (creator-led briefs only)**

- For some briefs, the creator submits a **script** first; the brand approves or requests changes.
- If the brief is **brand-led** (script already supplied), script review is skipped and the flow goes straight to **media**.

**Media**

- The creator uploads drafts (e.g. reel/story) through the workflow.
- A **72-hour review clock** starts when the brand receives a draft. If the brand does not approve or request changes within **72 hours**, the content is **auto-approved** and the process advances (including staging the **70%** payout path under escrow rules).

**Revision limit (“hard stop”)**

- The brand may request changes up to **two revision rounds** on the content.
- If the brand **rejects after the second round**, the collaboration enters a **termination** state:
  - The creator typically **keeps the 30% advance** as a kill fee.
  - The **70% balance** goes back to the brand.
  - The brand does **not** get usage rights to the disputed content.

**While reviewing**

- The brand sees deliverables, feedback fields, and approve / request revision actions.
- The creator sees status: awaiting review, revision requested, or approved.

---

### Step 5 — Publishing and compliance

**Goal:** Confirm the content is live on an allowed social platform before releasing the final payment (for awareness-style campaigns).

**Creator**

- Publishes on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube (as per brief).
- Submits the **live post URL** in the collaboration.

**Brand**

- Verifies the link is valid and on an allowed platform.
- For **awareness** objectives, the **70% final payment** stays locked until verification succeeds.
- For **production-only** objectives, link verification may be bypassed and settlement can proceed without a public post requirement.

**After verification**

- Escrow can release the remaining balance per policy.
- Manual brands may upload **final payment receipt** where required.

---

### Step 6 — Feedback and archive

**Goal:** Close the project professionally and retain a record.

**Reviews**

- Brand and creator can each leave a **rating and short review**.
- Reviews stay **hidden** until **both** have submitted or a **48-hour** window passes (double-blind style).

**Archive (“vault”)**

- The platform is intended to keep a permanent project record: agreement, assets, and payment receipts for both parties.

## Rules that protect both sides

| Rule | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Auto-approval (72 hours)** | Brands cannot stall creators indefinitely on drafts; silence approves after 72 hours. |
| **Negotiation lock** | At most one counter per side; then accept, final offer, or walk away. |
| **Logistics strike (2 failures)** | Repeated failed delivery or access triggers cancel path. |
| **Content hard stop (2 revisions)** | Third rejection ends the deal with advance kept by creator, no asset usage for brand. |
| **Usage rights** | Commercial use of content is only valid after compliance is verified and final payment rules are satisfied. |
| **Clean workspace** | Negotiation and quote screens only show rights and deliverables the brand enabled in the brief—reduces confusion and scope creep. |

## What appears in the chat

The conversation is the **audit trail** for the deal. Participants should expect:

- **System messages** (centered): step started, terms locked, funded, shipped, content approved, etc.
- **User messages**: normal back-and-forth.
- **Action cards** in the feed: e.g. “Proposed quote ₹X” with a button to open the execution panel and review.

Typical negotiation notifications:

| Event | What people see |
| --- | --- |
| Collaboration opens | Negotiation started; creator invited to quote |
| Creator sends quote | Amount proposed; brand to review |
| Brand counters | Counter amount; creator to accept or final-offer |
| Creator sends final offer | Final amount; brand must accept or decline |
| Terms agreed | Commercials locked; moving to securement |

---

## Brand vs creator: who acts when

| Step | Brand typically | Creator typically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Negotiation | Review quote, counter once, accept or decline | Submit quote, respond to counter, final offer |
| 2 Securement | Fund escrow or confirm manual payment | Add bank details, confirm advance received |
| 3 Logistics | Dispatch / share access | Confirm address, confirm receipt, report issues |
| 4 Content | Approve script/media or request revision | Submit script/media |
| 5 Publishing | Verify live URL, release final payment | Post and submit link |
| 6 Feedback | Rate and review | Rate and review |

At any moment, **one side usually “has the ball”**—the execution panel and chat call-to-action make that clear (e.g. “Review quote”, “Submit draft”, “Confirm receipt”).

---

## Paused, declined, or ended collaborations

- **Declined** during negotiation: the deal does not proceed; no securement.
- **Terminated** after content hard stop or logistics deadlock: collaboration stops; payment rules follow the scenario (advance retained or refunded as above).
- **Paused** campaigns (at campaign level) stop new applications but **active collaborations** can continue so brands can finish in-flight work.

---

## What success looks like

From a product perspective, the collaboration experience is working when:

1. **Money:** Final 70% is not released without the agreed compliance checks (where applicable).
2. **Creative:** No endless revision loops—two rounds, then a clear outcome.
3. **Logistics:** Repeated failed fulfillment does not trap either party in a dead deal.
4. **Transparency:** Every important action leaves a visible line in the chat for both brand and creator.
